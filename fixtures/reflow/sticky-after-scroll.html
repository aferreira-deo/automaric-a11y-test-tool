Annual report

The figures of the year, chapter by chapter.

IncomeSpendingStaff

Income

Income rose by four per cent over the year, most of it from the fees of the new services opened in the spring. Grants from the state were the same as the year before.

The sale of the old depot brought a one-off sum that has been set aside for the renovation of the schools, as the council decided in June.

Spending

Spending on roads went down after two years of heavy works, while spending on social care went up by six per cent with the number of people helped at home.

Energy costs were lower than planned thanks to the insulation of the public buildings and to a mild winter.

Staff

The council employed eleven hundred people at the end of the year, thirty more than a year before, most of them in care and in the schools.

Training hours went up by a fifth, with new courses on digital services and on first aid.

The report will be discussed at the public meeting of the council in March, where questions from residents are taken first.

The accounts have been checked by the auditors, whose report is published with this one.
