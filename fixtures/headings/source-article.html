Dredging the old harbour, one season at a time

By Jane Doe, 12 March

The silt came back faster than anyone had planned for, and the town had to decide whether the harbour was worth keeping open at all.

Why the channel fills up

Every spring the river carries sand down from the hills and drops it where the current slows, right at the harbour mouth.

The river

Most of the sediment arrives in three or four storms. The rest of the year the water runs clear.

The tide

Twice a day the tide pushes some of that sand back in. This is the part nobody measured. The old charts only show depth at low water.

We were dredging the same sand three times.

What the town tried

Three approaches were tested over five years, with mixed results.
